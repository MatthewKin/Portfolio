01 / OVERVIEW

Project Overview

Now You See It was developed for ZotJam Winter 2026, a 48-hour game jam. The short development period required quickly moving from an initial idea into a playable prototype and then refining the experience for the final submission.

With only 48 hours to work, development focused on establishing the game's core experience first before spending time on visuals, atmosphere, and polish.

The limited timeframe also meant making quick design decisions and prioritizing the elements that had the biggest impact on the player's experience.

02 / DEVELOPMENT

Development Process

01

DAY 1 — CONCEPT

The first part of the jam focused on establishing the game's main concept and figuring out how the core idea could be turned into a playable experience within the 48-hour timeframe.

02

DAY 1 — PROTOTYPE

After establishing the direction, development moved into prototyping the main gameplay systems. The priority was getting the core interaction working before focusing on presentation and polish.

03

DAY 1 — GAMEPLAY

The main gameplay systems were connected together and tested to make sure the core loop was playable. This provided a foundation for the rest of the development process.

04

DAY 2 — VISUALS

Once the core gameplay was working, development shifted toward the visual presentation. Art, effects, lighting, and environmental details were added to give the game a more distinct identity.

05

DAY 2 — ATMOSPHERE

More attention was given to the atmosphere of the game, using visual and audio elements to create a more cohesive experience. This helped strengthen the mood and presentation of the final build.

06

DAY 2 — POLISH

The final development period was focused on polishing the experience, fixing issues, and making adjustments based on testing. The goal was to make the final build feel cohesive despite the extremely short development window.

07

FINAL SUBMISSION

The completed build was prepared and submitted before the 48-hour deadline. The final result combined the core gameplay systems with the visual and atmospheric elements developed during the jam.

03 / VISUAL DEVELOPMENT

Visual Development

The visual development of Now You See It focused on creating a distinct atmosphere within the limited time available. Since the project was made in only 48 hours, visual decisions needed to be simple enough to implement quickly while still having a noticeable impact on the player's experience.

Lighting and visual effects were especially important for establishing the mood. Rather than only being used to make the environment easier to see, lighting helped control what the player focused on and how each area felt.

Darker areas could create a more mysterious or tense feeling, while areas with stronger lighting could draw attention to important parts of the environment. This helped make the atmosphere feel more intentional without requiring large amounts of additional art.

The final visual direction was built around making the most of a small number of visual elements. Lighting, effects, environments, and other presentation details worked together to give the game a stronger sense of identity.

04 / TECHNICAL DEVELOPMENT

Technical Development

The technical development of Now You See It focused on building the game's core gameplay systems quickly enough to leave time for visual development and polish.

Working within a 48-hour jam meant keeping the technical scope focused. I prioritized the systems that directly affected the player's experience and avoided adding unnecessary features that could take time away from the main gameplay loop.

This approach allowed the project to move from prototype to a complete playable build within the jam's limited timeframe.

05 / ITERATION

Iteration & Feedback

Iteration during the jam focused on making the core experience clearer and more cohesive. With only 48 hours available, changes needed to have a direct impact on gameplay or presentation.

Testing helped identify areas where the experience could be improved, allowing adjustments to be made before the final submission.

The final judging results also provided useful feedback on different aspects of the game, including ambience, theme relevance, fun and engagement, and creativity.

06 / FINAL RESULT

Final Result

Now You See It was completed for ZotJam Winter 2026, a 48-hour game jam. The game placed #4 in Ambience, #6 in Theme Relevance, and #7 Overall.

The project also received #10 in Fun & Engagement and #26 in Creativity. The jam gave me an opportunity to build a complete game under a very strict deadline while balancing gameplay, visuals, atmosphere, and polish.

One of the biggest challenges of the project was deciding where to spend the limited development time. The final result reflects the process of prioritizing the core experience first and then using the remaining time to strengthen the game's presentation and atmosphere.
