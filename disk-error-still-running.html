01 / OVERVIEW

Project Overview

DiskError: STILL RUNNING was created as a class group project by a team of three. The project required us to collaborate across multiple areas of game development while building a complete playable experience.

I worked as a generalist throughout development, contributing to the game's programming, pixel art, music, and audio. This allowed me to move between technical implementation and creative production depending on what the project needed.

Working across multiple disciplines also meant making sure the different parts of the project supported each other. Gameplay, visuals, music, and sound were developed together to create a more cohesive final experience.

02 / DEVELOPMENT

Development Process

01

CONCEPT & PLANNING

The project began with establishing the game's core concept and dividing responsibilities across the three-person team. We planned the main gameplay systems while identifying the visual and audio direction of the project.

02

PROTOTYPING

Development moved into prototyping the core gameplay systems. I focused on programming and getting the main interactions working before expanding the game's presentation.

03

GAMEPLAY DEVELOPMENT

I continued building and refining the game's systems while collaborating with the other members of the team. The gameplay was iterated on as the project became more complete.

04

ART & AUDIO

Alongside programming, I worked on pixel art, music, and sound effects. These elements were developed alongside the gameplay so the presentation could reinforce the game's overall identity.

05

ITERATION

The team tested the project and made adjustments to gameplay, visuals, and audio. My generalist role allowed me to move between different areas as problems and new needs came up.

06

POLISH

The final stage focused on refining the different pieces of the game and making sure the programming, pixel art, music, and sound effects worked together as a cohesive experience.

03 / VISUAL DEVELOPMENT

Visual Development

My visual contributions focused primarily on pixel art. I worked on creating and refining pixel-based assets used throughout the game while keeping the artwork consistent with the project's overall visual direction.

Because I was also responsible for programming, the art could be developed with the game's technical requirements in mind. This made it easier to iterate on assets and integrate them directly into the gameplay systems.

The visual work was developed alongside the game's other systems rather than being treated as a separate final step. This helped the presentation evolve together with the gameplay.

04 / TECHNICAL DEVELOPMENT

Technical Development

Programming was one of my primary responsibilities on DiskError: STILL RUNNING. I worked on implementing and connecting gameplay systems so that the different mechanics could function together as part of a complete game.

As a generalist, I also worked between the technical and creative sides of development. This meant considering how systems would interact with the pixel art, music, and sound effects being created for the project.

Working on multiple disciplines gave me a broader understanding of the development process and allowed me to contribute wherever the team needed additional support.

05 / ITERATION

Iteration & Feedback

Iteration was an important part of the project because the game was developed collaboratively. Testing allowed the team to identify issues with gameplay, visuals, and audio while continuing to refine the overall experience.

My role as a generalist allowed me to address issues across multiple areas of the project. I could move between programming, pixel art, music, and audio depending on what needed refinement.

This process helped the team keep the different disciplines connected rather than developing each part of the game independently.

06 / FINAL RESULT

Final Result

DiskError: STILL RUNNING was completed as a three-person class project. The experience allowed me to contribute across several disciplines, including programming, pixel art, music, and audio.

Working as a generalist gave me experience moving between different areas of game development while collaborating closely with a small team. It also gave me the opportunity to see how technical, visual, and audio elements can be developed together to create a complete game.
